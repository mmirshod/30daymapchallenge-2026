HOW TO READ THE LANDSCAPE

A model of a view.
A starting point for exploration.

Natural Panorama Score

The published NPS combines terrain (30%), visible peak proxies (25%), skyline (30%) and distance (15%). Components use cohort-relative percentiles and documented physical summaries, each on a 0–100 scale.

Changing the sliders recombines these precomputed components. It does not recalculate terrain visibility, normalization, or the internal definitions of the components.

Mountaineering Panorama Score

Published MPS = 80% NPS + 20% L. Horizon-only L combines visible landmark coverage (70%), distance (15%) and clipped positive angular relief (15%), with the same denominator of 15 for every observer. Self-observation is excluded.

Only Horizon is in scope. Mirage, Cascade and Underground are excluded. All five supplied Snow Leopard targets lie beyond 100 km. Program breadth is therefore omitted.

Know the limits

GDAL ground-target viewsheds on a 30 m Copernicus surface model are exploratory. A visible raster cell is not confirmed recognition of a summit. Vegetation, clouds and local terrain may change the real view. Skyline angular contrast is not topographic prominence.

The 100 km limit is projected; edge distances can slightly exceed 100 km geodesically. Elevation correlation is descriptive (Spearman 0.329), not causal. Nearly tied winners change with weights.

Profiles are displayed at 1° intervals, sampled from the original 0.1° calculations. Visible connections refer to DEM-ground point proxies. No weather or field visibility is implied.
